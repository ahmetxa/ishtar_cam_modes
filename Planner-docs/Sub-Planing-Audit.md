## 8. Content Quality and Implementability Analysis

**These sub-plans are specific, actionable, and unusually well suited to a coding agent.** That is not a courtesy; it is measurable in the documents. Acceptance criteria are numeric where a number exists (`black fraction comparable to 0.0024`, `4096 x 3072`, `0/12 trap keys`, `13 camera devices`, `mount counts 1/1`) and behavioural where no number exists, with the behaviour named precisely (`AGC launched without a DefaultErrorActivity and with no gcam.Gcam.f() NPE in the crash buffer`). Validation approaches name concrete command IDs bound to a contract table. Dependencies name specific sub-plans and specific ledger run ids rather than gesturing at "earlier work".

**The five new Phase 3 sub-plans set a higher bar than the corpus average, and it is worth being specific about why.**

- **They plan the measurement, not just the change.** `Faz3.7` requires the pass threshold for black fraction to be stated *before* the capture, and says so as an acceptance criterion. That closes the unfalsifiable-pass failure mode at the plan level rather than trusting the implementer.
- **They build the instrument before the repeated measurements.** `Faz3.8` sits between the premise and the two measurement-heavy sub-plans for a stated reason, and it is the same reasoning that pulled the smoke script forward into Phase 2. Consistency of reasoning across eight planning runs is rare.
- **They include a negative control and say why.** `Faz3.10` clears the pin deliberately to convert `R12` from inference into a measured signature. Most plans of this kind test only the happy path.
- **They ration the user's time explicitly.** `Faz3.10`'s "a snapshot per axis, a capture only where the pin changed, plus one confirming capture" is a stated policy with a stated cost basis, not an implicit one.
- **They refuse work.** `Faz3.10` declines to build a pin-enforcement mechanism because it would put a root-privileged writer into an application's private data for a problem whose frequency is not yet known. `Faz3.9` declines to redo `Faz3.4`'s consumer survey. `Faz3.11` declines to rewrite the journal. Each refusal names the sub-plan or principle that owns the declined work.

**Fitness for Step 4 task decomposition: high for 16 of the 17 non-complete sub-plans.** Work items are already IDed (`F3.7-01` … `F3.7-08`, `F4.2-01` … `F4.2-05`), each with a description and a named output, which is most of what Step 4's task files need. Files to modify are enumerated in the contract tables with a state. Validation commands are IDed. Execution order is fixed by index section 4c. Rollback is handled by the project's bracketing discipline — a smoke snapshot before and after with the comparable region diffed — rather than by per-task rollback notes, which is the right shape for this domain.

**Where the corpus is weaker, stated directly.**

- **`Faz4.4` was internally contradictory** and would have sent a careful implementer in a circle. Its section 1 amendment said "do not invent a substitute target"; its section 8 then handed them two criteria that cannot both be met and one that was the retired goal restated. This was the audit's P1, `AUDIT-FIX-17`. **Repaired 2026-09-04 by planning run 9:** section 8 now carries the per-camera-id array-count fact with no target attached, names the absence of `RAW_SENSOR` as a non-failure, and states that removing the broad rows is a regression to route back to `Faz4.3`. ## 9. Scope Drift and Architectural Consistency Analysis

**The `DEC-14` objective change is governed, not drift.** It originated with the user, is recorded in `Main-Planing.md` sections 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 7, 8 and 9, is registered as `SNAP-2026-09-03-MAIN` superseding `SNAP-2026-08-23-MAIN`, triggered the ledger's own section 7 replanning rule, produced `SNAP-2026-09-03-SUBPLANS`, and is narrated in index section 4c with an explicit statement of what it does and does not do. No phase was added, removed or renumbered. Six completed sub-plans were kept at `verified` rather than demoted, with the reasoning stated: their findings are what justified the change. This is the cleanest replanning event in the project's history and it should be read as the model for the next one.

**No new major phase was introduced and no major phase is missing.** Phase 3's heading changed; the phase itself did not move or split.

**Scope drift found: one, and it is an amendment gap rather than an expansion.** `Faz4.4` still carries the retired narrowing as an acceptance criterion. This is drift *backwards* — a sub-plan left standing on a premise the master plan has abandoned — which is the more dangerous direction, because it silently re-authorises retired work rather than adding new work a reader would notice.

**A second, milder gap: Phase 9's premise was not revisited.** Index section 4c explicitly limits itself to positions 9, 10 and amendment 4b, and states it does not touch positions 11–15. Phase 9 is position 13. But `DEC-14` changed what Phase 9 is supposed to claim and verify: the release checklist's capability claim, the corrected `module.prop` description, and `EV-23`'s disposition all depend on what AGC's working resolution *is*, which `DEC-14` redefined. The re-plan's boundary was drawn tightly and defensibly; the consequence outside that boundary was simply not recorded.

**Ownership of state: unchanged and correct.** The three layers — sensor driver, HAL, application — remain distinct across every sub-plan. `DEC-14` in fact *strengthens* the boundary: the replacement objective constrains AGC's resolution application-side through a preference the app already reads, explicitly rather than by removing sizes from the HAL, so no other camera consumer is affected. `Faz3.7` section 5 forbids any HAL patch, APK modification or smali change, and states the reason. `Faz3.8` forbids the smoke script from writing anything. The single-writer mode-file design is preserved.

**Tool-versus-core boundary: respected.** `scripts/smoke.sh` remains an instrument that reports and does not judge — `Faz3.8` makes that a scope constraint with a worked example of the violation it forbids (`agc.photo_size.ok` would be a verdict and would be wrong the moment the target changes). Patch tables remain offset-plus-md5 triples; `Faz4.3`'s consolidation is explicitly constrained by `halpatch.sh`'s two-state assumption rather than redesigning around it.

**Premature production or live activation: none.** No sub-plan publishes, pushes, or distributes. `Faz9.1` explicitly puts publishing out of scope. `Faz1.1` leaves the version-control remote as an open decision (`DEC-01`) precisely because a remote changes what may be committed. `Faz3.7` refuses to change the live HAL base until `DEC-15` is answered.

**Over-documentation: not present, and the balance is deliberately argued.** Of seventeen non-complete sub-plans, three are primarily documents (`Faz3.11`, `Faz9.3`, and half of `Faz9.2`) and each is tied to a measurement it records rather than to a general writing task. `Faz3.11` includes a read-back verification — set the state from the document alone and confirm the expected fields appear — which converts a document into something testable.

**Ontology consistency: preserved.** The vocabulary the index committed to at Step 2 holds across the new sub-plans: *sensor layer* / *HAL layer* / *application layer*; `fullres` as a HAL state distinct from `50mp` as a mode name; *mode* as the single-file runtime state; *patch table* as an offset-plus-md5 triple list. Invariants are cited by number and used correctly — invariant 4 (layer exclusion) in `Faz3.9`, invariant 7 (duration bucketing) in `Faz4.2`, invariant 8 (preference trap) in `Faz3.7` and `Faz3.8`, invariant 10 (offsets bound to one blob build) throughout Phase 4. `Faz4.7`'s supersession explicitly notes that nothing is lost because every located address is already in `Project-Ontology.md` section 6 — the ontology doing exactly the job it was created for.

**Planning-history continuity: strong.** Every new sub-plan cites the ledger `IMPL-*` rows it rests on, by number. `Faz3.7` builds on `IMPL-40` and `IMPL-43`; `Faz3.8` on `IMPL-04`, `IMPL-40` and `IMPL-43`; `Faz4.2`'s amendment on `IMPL-48` … `IMPL-50`. None duplicates completed work: `Faz3.9` explicitly refuses to redo `Faz3.4`'s survey, `Faz4.2` explicitly refuses to re-derive the recorded frame-rate measurements, and `Faz3.11` explicitly reads the ledger rows for `Faz3.7`–`Faz3.10` rather than re-reading the sub-plans. The ledger's Sub-Plan Status Matrix carries a row for all 40 sub-plans, with the five new ones seeded at `planned` under the new snapshot.

**Plan-history gaps that would confuse replanning: one small one.** Ledger section 6's Repository paragraph reads 28 commits / HEAD `de95ae3` / 171 tracked files, measured by `IMPL-34` on 2026-09-02. The tree now carries **46 commits, HEAD `83d4866`, 220 tracked files, `size-pack` 17.86 MiB**. ## 10. Readiness Realism

**The planning language distinguishes the levels correctly, and in several places better than the audit contract requires.**

- **Docs versus implementation.** `Faz3.11` is explicitly a document sub-plan and says so; it does not describe writing `docs/agc-12mp-configuration.md` as delivering the capability. Its acceptance criteria require a read-back — the state set from the document alone, the expected fields observed, one capture measured — which is the honest way to claim a document works.
- **Skeleton versus working runtime.** `Faz6.1` is `implemented` rather than `verified`, and the ledger states the distinction is the point: the edit is confirmed, the effect is not, and the effect verification is registered against Phase 7 rather than assumed. That is exactly right and it survived three audits without being quietly upgraded.
- **Local versus live readiness.** Every Phase 3 sub-plan separates the host-side measurement (`VAL-ARTIFACT`, PIL black fraction) from the device-side reads, and names which is which in section 9. `Faz4.2` names its stall-duration justification as weaker if `Faz3.5` did not produce a capture, and requires the weaker basis to be stated rather than hidden.
- **Smoke tests versus production confidence.** `scripts/smoke.sh` is consistently described as a state reporter, never as a pass. `Faz3.8` makes the refusal-to-judge a scope constraint.
- **Examples versus real configs.** No sub-plan treats a config example as a working credential. `Faz3.7` and `Faz3.11` both record that both root `.agc` files still carry all twelve trap key names and that importing either re-arms the trap — a config file treated as a hazard rather than as a setup step.
- **Pilot versus production core.** `Faz4.3` distinguishes the experimental candidate tables under `agc-50mp-yama/` from the shipped `hal-fullres.patch`, and `DEC-15` exists precisely because measuring on an experimental base and claiming it for the shipped artifact would be the classic overclaim. `Faz3.7` names its HAL md5 in every result for the same reason.

**Overclaims found: none in the new material.** The strongest claim any new sub-plan makes is scoped: `Faz3.7`'s goal is that AGC captures cleanly "while pinned", and its own risk section names the failure mode of reporting it without that qualifier. `Faz3.10` exists specifically to test the qualifier's durability. That is the correct shape.

**One retrospective honesty check the corpus passes.** `Faz3.6` closed the retired Phase 3 as "PRIMARY SUCCESS" while `FIND-3.5-01` (image corruption) stayed open, and neither the index nor the ledger later rewrote that into a failure. `DEC-14` retired the objective without invalidating a single completed sub-plan and says so explicitly. Keeping a recorded success that later proved insufficient, and explaining rather than erasing it, is the harder and better choice.

**Vibecoding language is not used as a substitute for slices.** Every "first vibecoding slice" in the corpus is a concrete, bounded action with a named output and a named validation signal. None is a strategy statement.

**One realism gap, and it was the P1; it is now closed.** `Faz4.4` claimed to validate distributability against criteria the shipped artifact cannot meet. A sub-plan whose pass condition is structurally unreachable is the readiness-realism failure mode in its purest form: it would have produced either a false failure or an invented target, and the plan explicitly forbids the second. The Step 3.1 repair removed the unreachable conditions rather than weakening them, so the sub-plan's pass condition is now the coexistence measurement it can actually take.

## 11. Security and Governance Findings

- **Secret safety: clean.** The bundled validator's length-bounded scan reports `secret_findings=0` across `Planner-docs/`. Worth watching rather than acting on.
- **Ledger or ontology assumptions that could create unsafe implementation behaviour: one, and it is `AUDIT-FIX-17`.** A sub-plan carrying a retired goal as an acceptance criterion is the mechanism by which a fresh session re-attempts abandoned work believing it is following the plan.
